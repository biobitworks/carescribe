CareScribe Room

What happened in there?

Julie started Leo’s evaluation, but it is not complete. Maya should still leave knowing what happened, what comes next, who owns it, and when.

Clinician-reviewed support · simulated data · never diagnosis

The problem

Two people leave the same room with unfinished work.

Julie · provider

Needs to preserve observations before they blur, finish the evaluation, and avoid keeping the next family waiting.

Maya · caregiver

Needs quick, actionable instructions—not a diagnosis—and clarity that the next visit is still evaluation.

One room · three roles

The conversation becomes a shared, reviewable plan.

1Maya

Controls capture, corrections, and sharing.

2Leo

May use words, gestures, actions, or loud vocalizations.

3Julie

Reviews evidence and retains clinical judgment.

The projector shows the reviewed story: transcript, advocate, evidence, uncertainty, and handoff.

Two audiences

Action for Maya. Evidence for Julie.

Caregiver Action Card
  • Today: evaluation started, not complete
  • Next: capture 2–3 HELP / MORE / STOP examples
  • Who: Maya observes; Julie reviews
  • When: before and at the second visit
Provider Evidence Card
  • Caregiver report stays attributed
  • Child signals keep uncertain meaning
  • Missing evidence stays explicit
  • Both people approve the exact handoff

The narrow promise

Preserve the family’s voice. Make the next step clear.

CareScribe does not decide what Leo’s observations mean. It gives Julie evidence and uncertainty to review, and gives Maya a clear, correctable handoff.
